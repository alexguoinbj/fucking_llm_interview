**1. SFT vs. RLHF: Core Issues and "Alignment"**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

![SFT (Behavior Cloning) vs. RLHF (Preference Alignment)](https://img.pricenode.uk/llm_interview_questions/english/chapter5/fig09_sft_vs_rlhf_core_issues_and_al.png)




SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) trains models by imitating "standard answers" annotated by experts, which is essentially **behavior cloning**. Its limitations include:

- **Distribution shift problem**: During training, the model only sees "good answers" written by annotators, but during inference, it faces the distribution generated by itself; once generation deviates from the training distribution, errors accumulate (exposure bias).
- **Lack of negative feedback signals**: SFT only tells the model "how to respond", but does not tell it "which responses are better/worse", making it unable to express human **preference rankings** and **degrees of difference**.
- **Inconsistency between objective function and human intent**: SFT optimizes token-level maximum likelihood, whereas being "helpful, harmless, and honest" represents higher-level goals that are difficult to exhaust with a single reference answer. The same question may have multiple reasonable expressions, but SFT forcibly forces the model to approach a single "standard answer", easily leading to mode collapse or rigid style.

RLHF models human preferences (rather than specific answers) by training a **reward model**, and then uses RL to let the policy model explore within a larger output space to find response distributions that achieve high rewards (i.e., better align with human preferences). This achieves a transformation from "imitation" to "optimizing human satisfaction", which is precisely the core of "alignment"—aligning the model's behavioral objectives with real human intent.

</details>

**2. Why Does RL After SFT Achieve Better Results?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

In modern LLM development, one of the most effective training paradigms is a two-stage process: performing Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) first, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL). The reasons why this combination is effective include:

- **Establishing a strong foundation through SFT**: SFT fine-tunes the model on high-quality data, providing a solid foundation, reducing the initial variance of model outputs, and setting a baseline distribution for subsequent optimization.
- **Safe and effective policy updates in RL**: The RL phase optimizes the policy by integrating a reward model reflecting human preferences, using mechanisms like clipping and KL divergence penalties to ensure policy updates remain close to the original SFT model.
- **Curriculum learning perspective**: The model first learns simpler, well-defined tasks through SFT, and then gradually tackles more complex decision-making challenges through RL.
- **Practical advantages**: SFT provides explicit guidance based on expert data, while RL allows optimizing long-term rewards and capturing nuances of human preferences.

</details>

**3. Why Not Perform Supervised Fine-Tuning Using Best-Generated Answers?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Even if a human or a reward model explicitly identifies a “best” answer, performing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on this answer has several drawbacks:
1. **Limited diversity and overfitting**: SFT typically involves training the model to imitate a single target output, which may cause the model to overfit to this narrow output and miss the full diversity of language.
2. **Sequential credit assignment**: In language generation tasks, reward signals are usually determined by the quality of the entire generated sequence. RL methods can assign credit across decision sequences, whereas purely supervised methods struggle to perform such subtle temporal credit assignment.
3. **Handling non-differentiable signals**: Many human feedback signals are non-differentiable; policy gradient methods can directly optimize expected rewards even when the reward function is non-differentiable.
4. **Exploration and exploitation**: RL inherently includes an exploration component, encouraging the model to sample and evaluate multiple trajectories, leading to a more robust policy.

</details>

**4. What Are the Advantages of Exploring LLM Training via RL?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

A significant benefit of integrating reinforcement learning into the LLM training pipeline is its inherent enhancement of generalization capabilities and the ability to handle out-of-distribution scenarios.

- **Broad exploration of the output space**: Unlike supervised fine-tuning that trains models on a fixed set of target responses, RL emphasizes exploration. During training, the model is exposed to a wide range of outputs through sampling and policy gradient updates.
- **Learning relative advantage**: RL methods compute an advantage estimate for each trajectory, which is essentially a relative metric comparing how much better a sequence is than average results.
- **Robustness to novel inputs**: When a model is trained solely via supervised learning, it may overfit to the training data. In contrast, the exploration inherent in RL means the model is regularly exposed to diverse inputs and outputs.
- **Improvement through exploration**: It is often during the exploration phase that the model discovers better trajectories than those observed in the supervised dataset.

</details>

**5. What is the Overall Pipeline of RLHF?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

![The Three Stages of the Classic RLHF Pipeline](https://img.pricenode.uk/llm_interview_questions/english/chapter5/fig10_what_is_the_overall_pipeline_o.png)




RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) is currently the mainstream method for large model alignment. Its complete pipeline is typically divided into three stages:

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
- Fine-tune the pre-trained model on high-quality (instruction, response) pair data in a supervised manner, enabling the model with basic instruction-following capabilities;
- Purpose: Provide a good policy initialization for subsequent RL training—if RL is performed starting from random initialization, the reward signal would be extremely sparse and exploration efficiency very low.

**Stage 2: Reward Model Training**
- For the same prompt, let the SFT model generate multiple candidate responses;
- Human annotators rank preferences over these responses, generating pairwise preference data $(y_w, y_l)$;
- Train the reward model $r_\phi$ using the Bradley-Terry model: $Loss = -\log\sigma(r_\phi(y_w) - r_\phi(y_l))$;
- The reward model is essentially a "differentiable proxy for human preferences", providing differentiable reward signals for subsequent RL.

**Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning Optimization (PPO Fine-Tuning)**
- Initialize the Actor with the SFT model, and use the reward model $r_\phi$ to provide reward signals;
- PPO algorithm optimization objective: Maximize rewards while constraining the deviation from the SFT model via KL divergence: $Objective = \mathbb{E}[r_\phi(y)] - \beta \cdot KL(\pi_\theta || \pi_{SFT})$;
- Four models are involved in training: Actor (under training), Critic (under training), Reward Model (frozen), and Reference Model (frozen).

**Overall Design Logic**: SFT solves the "cold start", RM solves "preference quantification", and PPO solves "policy optimization". All three stages are indispensable, jointly realizing the model's transition from "knowing how to speak" to "saying what humans want to hear".

</details>

**6. What is RLHF? Briefly Outline Its Training Pipeline.**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

**RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)** is an alignment training method that makes model outputs better conform to human preferences.
**The training pipeline consists of three steps**:
1. **SFT**: Perform supervised fine-tuning on the pre-trained model using high-quality human-annotated data to obtain the SFT model.
2. **Reward Model Training**: Have the SFT model generate multiple responses for the same prompt, manually rank preferences over these responses, and use the ranking data to train a reward model capable of scoring and evaluating response quality.
3. **PPO Reinforcement Learning**: Use scores from the reward model as feedback signals to optimize the SFT model with the PPO algorithm, while incorporating KL divergence constraints to prevent the model from deviating too far from the original distribution.

</details>

**7. Compared with Traditional SFT, What Core Issues in Language Models Does RLHF Aim to Solve? Why Is SFT Alone Insufficient to Achieve Our Desired "Alignment" Goal?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Compared with traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) aims to address deeper "alignment" issues in language models. Specifically, this includes three aspects, commonly referred to as the "HHH" principles:

1. Helpfulness: The model should provide accurate, relevant, and informative content, doing its best to help users solve problems.
2. Honesty: The model should answer based on its knowledge without fabricating facts. When it does not know the answer or cannot satisfy a request, it should proactively admit so rather than hallucinate.
3. Harmlessness: The model must not generate biased, discriminatory, violent, pornographic, or any other content that could cause harm.

Why is SFT alone insufficient to achieve alignment goals?

1. Vague goal definitions: Concepts like "helpful", "honest", and "harmless" are complex, subjective, and context-dependent, making them difficult to precisely define via a static, fixed SFT dataset. For example, "what constitutes a helpful response?" has no single correct answer; it depends on user preferences.
2. Difficult preference annotation: For a single prompt, there may be multiple "correct" responses differing in style, granularity, and focus. SFT typically uses data formats like (prompt, ideal_response), which cannot express fine-grained preference information such as "response A is better than response B".
3. Vast behavioral space: LLMs can generate virtually infinite responses. An SFT dataset can only cover a tiny fraction of high-quality examples, making it easy for the model to learn surface statistical artifacts in the dataset rather than truly understanding the underlying principles. It teaches the model to "imitate", but not to "judge".
4. Exposure Bias: During training, SFT bases every step on the true "golden" context. However, during inference, the model generates based on its own previously generated context; once an early deviation occurs, errors accumulate.

By introducing a reward model representing human preferences, RLHF enables the LLM to learn within an exploratory framework (reinforcement learning), allowing it to comprehend and optimize vague human preferences that are difficult to express under the SFT paradigm, thereby achieving better alignment.

</details>

**8. Elaborate in Detail on the Three Core Stages of the Classic RLHF Pipeline. For Each Stage, What Are the Inputs, Outputs, and Key Goals?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

![Detailed Inputs, Outputs, and Model Transitions in RLHF Stages](https://img.pricenode.uk/llm_interview_questions/english/chapter5/fig11_elaborate_in_detail_on_the_thr.png)




The classic RLHF pipeline (proposed in OpenAI's InstructGPT paper) consists of three core stages:

Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

- Input: A high-quality, human-written or curated instruction-following dataset. The data format is typically (Instruction Prompt, Ideal Response).
- Output: A fine-tuned base language model, referred to as the SFT model.
- Key Goal: Enable the pre-trained LLM to initially gain the ability to understand and follow human instructions. This serves as the foundation for providing a good initial policy for subsequent stages, ensuring the model first learns "what to say" rather than generating nonsensical outputs.

Stage 2: Reward Model (RM) Training

- Input: A human preference comparison dataset. The workflow for generating this dataset is:

1. Sample a prompt from the instruction dataset.
2. Use the SFT model from Stage 1 to generate multiple (typically 2 to 4) different responses for the prompt.
3. Human annotators rank these responses to select the winning and losing ones. The data format is typically (Prompt, Winning Response $y_w$, Losing Response $y_l$).

- Output: A reward model (RM). This model accepts any (Prompt, Response) pair as input and outputs a scalar score representing the degree of human preference for that response.
- Key Goal: Learn a function capable of imitating and generalizing human preferences. This RM serves as the "environment" or "judge" for the next reinforcement learning stage, providing guidance signals for the LLM's exploration.

Stage 3: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)

- Input:

1. The SFT model from Stage 1 (serving as the initial policy).
2. The trained RM from Stage 2 (serving as the reward function).
3. A new instruction dataset used for policy exploration.

- Output: The final language model aligned via RLHF.
- Key Goal: Use reinforcement learning to further fine-tune the SFT model. In this phase, the model (acting as the Agent) generates a response (Action) for a given prompt, and the reward model (acting as the Environment) scores this response (Reward). Parameters are then updated via the PPO algorithm so that the generated responses receive high rewards while not straying too far from the style and content of the original SFT model, thereby achieving "alignment".

</details>

**9. What Datasets Are Used in RLHF?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

The three stages of RLHF require different types of datasets, and data quality directly determines the upper bound of alignment performance.

**1. SFT Dataset (Supervised Fine-Tuning Stage)**
- **Format**: (Instruction/Question, High-quality reference response) pairs;
- **Characteristics**: Requires high quality and diversity, covering various tasks (QA, creative writing, code, dialogue, etc.);
- **Sources**: Human annotation, filtering from the internet, distillation from stronger models;
- **Typical Examples**: Approx. 13k human-annotated data points used by InstructGPT; public datasets such as ShareGPT and Alpaca.

**2. Preference Pair Dataset (Reward Model Training Stage)**
- **Format**: (prompt, $y_{chosen}$, $y_{rejected}$) triplets;
- **Characteristics**: Requires diverse comparison scenarios, including hard-to-distinguish "near pairs" (highly informative) and clearly different "easy pairs";
- **Annotation Method**: Human annotators compare two or more responses and select the one that better adheres to the HHH (Helpful, Harmless, Honest) principles;
- **Typical Examples**: Anthropic HH-RLHF, OpenAI preference dataset, HelpSteer, etc.

**3. Prompt Dataset for PPO Training (Reinforcement Learning Stage)**
- **Format**: Contains only prompts without reference responses;
- **Role**: Serves as input for rollouts, allowing the model to autonomously generate responses scored by the reward model to drive policy updates;
- The volume is much larger than the previous two types, typically reaching tens to hundreds of thousands of prompts.

**4. Evaluation Datasets**

Held-out test sets used for independently evaluating the performance of each stage, covering multiple dimensions such as safety, helpfulness, and honesty.

Quality relationship among the three types of training data: Preference Data Quality $>$ SFT Data Quality $>$ Prompt Quantity. The first two determine the ceiling of alignment performance.

</details>

**10. What Are the Advantages of RL Compared to SFT?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Reinforcement learning (RL) offers unique advantages over supervised fine-tuning (SFT) across multiple core dimensions, which serves as the fundamental driving force for the evolution of post-training pipelines from SFT to RL:

**1. Breaking Beyond Demonstrator Limits**

SFT is essentially imitation learning, where the upper bound of model performance is restricted by the best demonstration responses in the training data. RL requires only scalar feedback on outcomes. Through autonomous exploration, RL can discover superior solutions that never appeared in the training data—especially in tasks with objective verification standards like math and code, where the model can discover solution pathways exceeding human demonstrators through trial and verification.

**2. Holistic Sequence Optimization**

SFT computes an independent cross-entropy loss for each token, treating every token in the response as equally important. RL optimizes based on the final reward of the entire response, enabling it to learn "which combinations of tokens produce good overall results," thereby assigning greater weight to critical steps in the reasoning process.

**3. Higher Data Efficiency**

SFT requires high-quality annotated (prompt, response) pairs, which are scarce and expensive. RL requires only prompts and reward signals—for verifiable tasks (math answer correctness, code unit test pass rates), massive volumes of reward signals can be obtained automatically, significantly lowering data costs.

**4. Exploring New Reasoning Patterns**

RL encourages the model to try different reasoning paths via high-temperature sampling, and then reinforces effective paths using reward signals. This "exploration-reinforcement" mechanism allows high-level reasoning behaviors—such as reflection, self-verification, and multi-method trial—to **spontaneously emerge**, which is difficult to achieve with SFT.

**5. Direct Optimization of Target Metrics**

SFT optimizes log-likelihood, which does not fully align with ultimate task metrics (e.g., math accuracy, code pass rate, human satisfaction). RL can directly optimize rewards most closely tied to task goals, narrowing the gap between proxy objectives and true objectives.

</details>

**11. Why Is RLHF Indispensable for Large Language Models?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Although SFT is effective, it suffers from several fundamental limitations that make RLHF indispensable for achieving true safety alignment and high-quality outputs in LLMs:

**1. SFT Cannot Truly Distinguish "Good" from "Bad"**

The training signal of SFT is to "make the model's output distribution closely match the responses in the training data," treating high-quality and low-quality responses indiscriminately—as long as a sample is in the training data, its likelihood is maximized. This means SFT cannot enable the model to truly understand "what kind of responses are preferred by humans"; it can only learn "what kind of responses appear in the training set."

**2. Limitations of SFT Data**

- **Limited and Fixed Quantity**: High-quality SFT data is expensive to acquire, with quantities far smaller than pre-training corpora;
- **Fixed Distribution**: Once SFT data collection is complete, the model is locked into this fixed distribution, making it hard to adapt to the diverse needs of real users;
- **Single-Correct-Answer Assumption**: SFT assumes each prompt corresponds to a single "standard response," whereas many questions lack a unique correct answer and human preferences are pluralistic.

**3. Inability to Capture Nuances in Human Preferences**

Human judgment of response quality involves balancing multiple dimensions such as helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty. Such multi-dimensional subjective preferences are difficult to fully express via the single cross-entropy loss of SFT. RLHF captures these multi-dimensional preferences by explicitly training a reward model, and then uses RL to directly optimize the policy model to maximize this preference function, aligning more accurately with true human intent.

**4. Insufficient Safety Guarantees**

Models trained purely via SFT often fail to guarantee safe outputs when confronted with adversarial inputs or edge cases. RLHF explicitly rewards safe behaviors and penalizes unsafe ones through the reward model, providing a systematic mechanism for safety alignment.

</details>

**12. Why Do RLHF Models Outperform Pure SFT Models?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

![Model-Free RL Policy vs Value Updates](https://img.pricenode.uk/llm_interview_questions/english/chapter5/fig12_why_do_rlhf_models_outperform.png)




RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) can further enhance model performance on top of SFT, primarily due to the following reasons:

**1. SFT Is Constrained by the Inherent Limitations of "Imitation Learning"**

- SFT essentially asks the model to imitate the "standard answers" given in the training data; the model learns to "output content as close as possible to the target answer given an input." However, annotated answers are often just **one** of many "acceptable" responses. In pursuit of token-by-token matching, the model may acquire unnecessary, overly "templated" expressions;
- SFT data inherently provides only **positive examples** (what should be done), lacking **relative comparison** signals regarding "which outputs are better and which are worse." Consequently, the model struggles to learn fine-grained quality distinctions such as "among multiple possible, reasonable responses, which one is preferred."

**2. RLHF Introduces the Richer Training Signal of "Relative Preference"**

- By training a reward model (based on human relative preference rankings over multiple candidate responses) and then using reinforcement learning to optimize the policy model to maximize reward, RLHF enables the model to acquire **richer quality gradient information than merely "imitating a single reference answer"**—the reward model can score diverse outputs sampled from the model itself, allowing the policy to adjust its generation distribution toward "more human-preferred" directions without being confined to reproducing fixed target text.

**3. Exploration and Exploitation: Learning from the Model's "Own Output Distribution"**

- During the RLHF stage, samples generated by the policy model come from **the model's own output distribution** (on-policy or approximate on-policy), and training signals directly target "what the model actually generates." In contrast, SFT training signals come from "how annotators write." A mismatch may exist between these two distributions (**distribution mismatch**)—SFT trains the model to fit a distribution that might not naturally arise when the model samples on its own, whereas RLHF optimizes directly on the model's own output distribution, addressing the root cause more effectively.

**4. Better Alignment with Overall Human Preference (Rather Than Local Token-Level Matching)**

- SFT loss is token-level cross-entropy, which fundamentally encourages "local" matching (making each step's prediction as close as possible to the target). However, the "overall quality" of a text (such as helpfulness, safety, or stylistic adherence) is a **holistic/sequence-level** attribute that token-level loss may not adequately capture; reward signals in RLHF are typically provided for the **entire output sequence**, directly optimizing the target of "overall quality."

**5. Empirical Performance in Practice**

- Post-RLHF models generally show significant improvements over pure SFT models in **instruction-following precision, response helpfulness, safety (appropriateness in refusing harmful requests), and overall win rates in human evaluations**. Multiple studies and practical applications (such as the InstructGPT paper) have shown that RLHF-aligned models achieve significantly higher win rates in human evaluations than models trained only with SFT, even when the latter have substantially larger parameter counts.

**Summary**: The core reason RLHF achieves superior performance is that it introduces **sequence-level training signals based on relative preferences** and optimizes directly on the model's own output distribution. This compensates for the fine-grained signal insufficiency and distribution mismatch inherent in SFT's "token-level loss aimed at imitating fixed annotated text," enabling the model to adjust more precisely toward "overall output quality genuinely preferred by humans."

</details>

**13. What Is the Difference Between Modern Reinforcement Learning and Classic RLHF?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Next-generation reinforcement learning, represented by DeepSeek-R1, exhibits significant differences in design philosophy and technical implementation compared to classic RLHF, represented by InstructGPT:

**1. Fundamental Differences in Reward Sources**
- **Classic RLHF**: Relies on a **Reward Model (RM)** trained on human preferences to provide subjective reward signals—essentially a "simulation of human satisfaction"—which inherently carries subjectivity and noise;
- **Modern RL**: Prioritizes **rule-based verifiable rewards**—objective standards such as mathematical answer correctness or code unit test pass rates, virtually eliminating the Reward Hacking problem.

**2. Differences in Core Objectives**
- **Classic RLHF**: The primary goal is to align with "human preferences"—making model responses more helpful, harmless, and honest;
- **Modern RL**: The primary goal is to enhance **actual reasoning capability**—enabling the model to engage in "slow thinking," explore and reinforce effective reasoning paths, and pursue performance breakthroughs on objective tasks.

**3. Algorithmic Evolution**
- Classic RLHF primarily uses PPO (requiring 4 models);
- Modern RL increasingly adopts GRPO (eliminating the Critic, requiring 3 models), or further simplified variants like ReMax and Dr.GRPO.

**4. Degree of Reliance on SFT**
- Classic RLHF typically requires high-quality SFT as a cold start;
- DeepSeek R1-Zero proved that for certain tasks, one can **skip SFT and perform large-scale RL directly**, allowing reasoning capabilities to emerge spontaneously.

Summary: Modern RL is closer to the "outcome-driven exploration" paradigm of traditional reinforcement learning, rather than the "preference fitting" paradigm of classic RLHF.

</details>

**14. What Type of Reinforcement Learning Is Used in Post-Training?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Reinforcement learning in the LLM post-training stage possesses several key characteristics that distinguish it from traditional game/control RL:

**1. Policy-Based Methods**

Post-training primarily uses methods that **directly optimize policy parameters** (e.g., PPO, GRPO) rather than value-based methods (e.g., Q-Learning, DQN). Reason: The action space of an LLM is a discrete vocabulary (tens of thousands of dimensions), making it intractable to calculate Q-values for every state-action pair; policy gradient methods optimize directly in the policy space and are naturally suited for this setting.

**2. Model-Free Methods**

Instead of learning a transition model of the environment $P(s'|s,a)$, these methods optimize the policy directly by interacting with the environment (reward models or rule verifiers), thereby simplifying the training pipeline.

**3. Primarily Online, Complemented by Offline**
- PPO/GRPO are Online methods, requiring real-time rollout sampling;
- Preference optimization methods such as DPO/IPO are Offline methods, utilizing pre-collected datasets;
- Iterative DPO represents a trade-off between the two.

**4. Proximal On-Policy**

PPO achieves "proximal on-policy" learning via a clipping mechanism—allowing bounded reuse of off-policy data (to improve efficiency) while correcting the optimization direction via importance sampling. This is an engineering compromise between pure on-policy (very low sample efficiency) and completely off-policy learning (severe data distribution shift).

**5. Actor-Critic or Critic-Free Frameworks**

PPO employs an Actor-Critic architecture: the Actor generates tokens, and the Critic estimates state values. GRPO eliminates the Critic, estimating advantages via the relative rewards of multiple sampled outputs within a group, thereby reducing GPU memory requirements and engineering complexity.

</details>

**15. What Are the Common Algorithms in Post-Training?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

LLM post-training aims to align pre-trained models with human intent. The main algorithm families are as follows:

**1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
- **Standard SFT**: Standard cross-entropy training on (instruction, response) pairs, serving as the foundational phase of post-training;
- **Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT)**: Samples multiple outputs per prompt and retains correct samples for SFT;
- **Expert Iteration**: Generates candidate answers with the model, filters correct answers for SFT, and iteratively improves performance.

**2. Preference Optimization (Offline RL)**
- **DPO**: Derives a closed-form loss function directly from the PPO+RM objective, eliminating the need for an explicit RM;
- **IPO**: An improvement on DPO that prevents chosen and rejected probabilities from decreasing simultaneously;
- **KTO**: Requires no paired data, directly using binary good/bad labels for single responses;
- **ORPO**: Unifies SFT loss and preference loss, eliminating the need for a reference model.

**3. Reinforcement Learning (Online RL)**
- **PPO**: The core algorithm of classic RLHF, requiring 4 models and offering robust training;
- **GRPO**: Eliminates the Critic and uses intra-group reward normalization, adopted by DeepSeek-R1;
- **REINFORCE++/ReMax**: Simplified REINFORCE variants that eliminate the Critic;
- **RLAIF**: Uses AI models instead of human annotators to provide preference signals.

**4. Other Methods**
- **Best-of-N (BoN)**: Samples $N$ outputs during inference and selects the one with the highest reward; requires no training but incurs high inference costs;
- **STaR**: Iterative SFT using self-generated rationale chain data.

Current Mainstream Pipeline: SFT Cold Start $\rightarrow$ GRPO (Reasoning Tasks) or DPO (General Alignment) $\rightarrow$ Iterative Optimization.

</details>

**16. What Other Post-Training Alignment Methods Exist Outside of RLHF?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Besides the complete RLHF pipeline (SFT + RM + PPO), several simpler or alternative post-training alignment methods exist:

**1. DPO and Its Variants (No Explicit RM Needed)**

DPO directly derives a loss function for the policy model from the PPO+RM optimization objective, training directly on preference data to avoid the overhead of training a separate reward model and performing online rollouts. Variants include IPO, KTO (no paired data needed), and ORPO (combining SFT and preference objectives).

**2. Best-of-N Sampling (BoN)**

Generates $N$ candidate responses at inference time and selects the best one using a reward model or rule-based verifier. It requires no modifications to model parameters and is simplest to implement, but inference cost scales linearly with $N$; it is often used as an evaluation baseline.

**3. Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT / STaR)**

Prompts the model to generate multiple answers for each question, retaining correct/high-quality answers for SFT. Iterative updates (new model generates better answers $\rightarrow$ filtering $\rightarrow$ SFT again) progressively enhance model capabilities. This requires no RM, but is only applicable to tasks with objective correct answers.

**4. RLAIF (AI Feedback)**

Replaces human annotators with large models (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) to provide preference signals, training an RM before performing RL or DPO. This significantly reduces annotation costs, but introduces the risk of AI bias.

**5. Constitutional AI**

Proposed by Anthropic: uses a set of preset "principles" (a constitution) to guide the model in self-critique and revision of its responses, then uses these revised data for SFT and RL, achieving interpretable alignment.

**6. Direct Inference-Time Alignment**

Guides outputs without modifying model parameters, instead relying on improved prompting (System Prompts, Chain-of-Thought) or inference-time decoding strategies (e.g., Contrastive Decoding). This approach has the lowest cost, but its maximum effectiveness is bounded by the base model's inherent capabilities.

</details>

**17. What Methods Are Typically Used in the Post-Training Stage Prior to Reinforcement Learning?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

Prior to the reinforcement learning phase, post-training typically undergoes the following steps:

**1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)—The Core Prerequisite Stage**

SFT is an indispensable foundation for reinforcement learning. Supervised training is conducted on a pre-trained base model using high-quality human-annotated (instruction, response) data, enabling the model to:
- Learn dialogue formatting (transitioning from predicting arbitrary next tokens to generating structured assistant responses);
- Acquire basic instruction-following capabilities (understanding prompts such as "Please write an essay about...");
- Provide a reasonable policy initialization point for RL—if RL were performed directly on a pre-trained base model, the exploration space would be excessively large and reward signals extremely sparse.
SFT datasets typically range from a few thousand to tens of thousands of samples, emphasizing quality over quantity.

**2. DPO or Preference Warm-up (Optional)**

Some training pipelines insert a round of DPO training after SFT and before reinforcement learning, leveraging pre-collected preference data for preliminary alignment:
- Rapidly improves model safety and helpfulness without relying on online rollouts;
- Provides a better policy starting point for subsequent PPO/GRPO, accelerating convergence during the RL phase.

**3. Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT, Optional)**

For tasks with objective evaluation standards (e.g., mathematics, coding), RFT can be added after SFT—letting the model generate multiple candidates and retaining correct ones for SFT, further elevating baseline capabilities before entering the RL phase.

Summary: The standard pipeline is **SFT (required) $\rightarrow$ DPO/RFT (optional) $\rightarrow$ RL (PPO/GRPO)**, where SFT lays the foundation of "knowing how to speak" for reinforcement learning.

</details>

**18. Reasons and Perspectives on the Industry Shift from SFT to RL**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

The industry is gradually transitioning from SFT-dominated post-training to RL-assisted post-training, driven by multiple technical and practical factors:

**1. Core Limitations of SFT**
- **Upper bound constrained by annotated data**: SFT is essentially imitation learning, where the model's performance ceiling is determined by the best responses in the training data. High-quality annotated data is scarce and expensive, forming a performance ceiling for SFT;
- **Inability to surpass the demonstrator**: SFT only allows the model to "learn known good answers" and cannot discover superior solutions that human annotators never provided;
- **Lack of credit assignment**: SFT applies uniform token-level supervision across the entire response, failing to distinguish critical steps from irrelevant content within a response.

**2. Unique Advantages of RL**
- **Outcome-oriented, surpassing human limits**: RL requires only "correct/incorrect" or "good/bad" signals without needing demonstrations of the correct process. For verifiable tasks such as mathematics and coding, the model can discover problem-solving paths that humans have not thought of through autonomous exploration;
- **Leveraging massive unannotated prompts**: RL requires only prompts without human-annotated responses, significantly reducing data costs and allowing the use of broader user query data;
- **Emergence of multi-step reasoning capabilities**: Works like DeepSeek-R1-Zero demonstrate that RL can trigger the autonomous emergence of high-order reasoning behaviors such as self-reflection and self-verification, which are difficult to achieve via SFT.

**3. Mainstream Views in the Industry**
- SFT and RL are not mutually exclusive but complementary—SFT provides a good initialization, upon which RL further optimizes;
- For tasks with objectively verifiable rewards (math, code, tool calls), the improvements from RL are highly significant, which has largely become an industry consensus;
- For highly subjective general conversation tasks, the effectiveness of RL depends more heavily on the quality of the reward model, making simplified schemes like DPO reasonable choices as well.

</details>

**19. In Which Training Stage Are LLM Reasoning Capabilities Generally Developed? (Why Is It Said That "SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes"?)**

**The Meaning and Reasons Behind "SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes"**:

The core thesis of this viewpoint (widely discussed in works such as DeepSeek-R1) is as follows:

**1. The SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) Stage—"Memory / Imitation"**

- The training signal of SFT is "imitating annotated reasoning processes/answers"—the model learns to "output text that matches the given problem-solving steps/answers in the training data as closely as possible for a given prompt";
- This learning method is essentially **the memorization and imitation of reasoning patterns/problem-solving paths appearing in the training data**—the model may learn to "reproduce similar problem-solving steps on seen problem types," but **for unseen problem types or new reasoning paths, the model lacks a mechanism to generalize "universal reasoning capabilities" from mere imitation**;
- The token-by-token cross-entropy loss of SFT encourages the model to match the annotated tokens at every step. There is a gap between this "local matching" optimization objective and the goal of "learning a general reasoning strategy/mindset applicable to novel problems."

**2. The RL Stage—"Generalization / Exploration"**

- The training signal of RL (such as GRPO combined with outcome-based rewards) is **"whether the final result is correct."** The model can **autonomously explore/sample different reasoning paths**; as long as it arrives at the correct answer, it receives a positive reward regardless of the specific intermediate reasoning steps;
- This "outcome-oriented, exploration-permitting" training mode gives the model the opportunity to **discover and reinforce reasoning strategies/patterns that are truly effective and generalizable** (rather than being restricted to imitating a fixed annotated path). The model **autonomously "discovers"** effective reasoning behaviors during RL training (for instance, as observed in the DeepSeek-R1 paper, behaviors such as "reflection," "self-verification," and "trying multiple approaches" spontaneously emerged during RL training; these behaviors did not originate from explicit SFT annotations, but naturally emerged during the RL optimization for final accuracy);
- Because the optimization objective of RL aligns more directly with final task performance (rather than imitating a specific intermediate process), and because the model optimizes over **its own output distribution** (exploring which of the multiple reasoning paths it generates are more effective), this training paradigm is more conducive to forming **essential reasoning capabilities/strategies transferable to new problems**, rather than memorizing specific solutions to specific problems.

**3. The Training Stage Where Reasoning Capabilities Emerge**

- Basic reasoning "patterns/formats" (such as step-by-step thinking or using specific notation) may already be partially acquired during the **pre-training stage** (since large-scale corpora contain vast amounts of text with explicit reasoning processes);
- The **SFT stage** allows the model to "learn" to output reasoning processes in a desired format/style (e.g., an explicit chain-of-thought format), serving to "activate/guide" the model to exhibit existing capabilities, but its contribution to "improving reasoning capabilities per se (especially generalizing to novel problems)" is relatively limited;
- The **RL stage (especially large-scale RL based on verifiable outcome rewards)** is widely considered the **key stage for significantly enhancing model reasoning capabilities, particularly generalized reasoning on novel and complex problems**—through the cycle of "exploration-verification-reinforcement," the model gradually discovers and consolidates genuinely effective reasoning strategies.

**Summary**: Basic reasoning "formats / nascent capabilities" may originate from pre-training, while the SFT stage serves primarily for "format alignment / capability activation" (considered to lean more toward "memorization and imitation" of existing problem-solving paths). In contrast, the **RL stage (based on outcome-oriented rewards that allow the model to autonomously explore diverse reasoning paths and reinforce effective strategies) is recognized as the critical phase for significantly boosting LLM reasoning capabilities (especially generalization to new problems)**. The saying "SFT memorizes, RL generalizes" succinctly captures this difference: SFT tends to make the model "memorize" specific patterns in annotated data, whereas RL enables the model to "discover and generalize" genuinely effective and transferable reasoning strategies.
