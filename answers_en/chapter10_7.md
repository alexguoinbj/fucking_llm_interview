# 10.7 AI Technology Trends and Product Judgment — Questions & Answers
> [⬅ Back to Index](../README_EN.md) · Chapter 10: LLM Product Deployment and Commercialization · 4 questions

**1. Is "Agent" a Fake Demand?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

"Agent" as a technology paradigm is not inherently a false need—automated execution of multi-step tasks indeed provides genuine value. However, the issue with many "false need" products lies in treating "wrapping a simple process with an Agent" as an innovation, when that process could have been accomplished using simpler and more reliable traditional automation, lacking scenarios where Agents truly excel (handling uncertainty and dynamic decision-making).

</details>

**2. Is "MCP" a Fake Demand?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

The problem of "tool integration standardization" addressed by MCP is a genuine engineering pain point. However, for many end users, MCP itself is underlying infrastructure; what users perceive is "whether a task can be accomplished," rather than the protocol itself. Its value should be judged by whether it effectively lowers integration costs and promotes ecosystem interoperability.

</details>

**3. Is "Multi-Agent" a Fake Demand?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

For genuinely complex and decomposable task scenarios, Multi-Agent architecture has its value. However, if a simple task is forcibly split into multi-Agent collaboration merely to "look more sophisticated," the added complexity and cost will not yield a corresponding performance improvement, rendering such applications closer to a false need.

</details>

**4. Will the Advancement of Long-Context Technology Render RAG Obsolete?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Show Answer</summary>

(Echoing previous points) It will not completely lose its value, but it will shift the boundaries of RAG's applicability. For scenarios where the volume of knowledge fits within the long-context capacity and updates infrequently, directly utilizing long contexts may be simpler. However, RAG remains irreplaceable in scenarios involving ultra-large-scale data, high real-time requirements, and precise source attribution.
